The recovered query-to-output constraints are:

1. The interpreter starts with the original question. It describes sought content and produces semantic tags, without inventing answer facts or receiving source bodies, corpus inventories or gold. Keep the original question available when interpreting that description.
2. Query facets describe each tag's relevance to the sought content through each facet. They are not tag categories or a universal facet-importance vector. Whether and how these readings become sorting priorities or numerical contributions is explicit, not silently inherited from the older part-order interpreter.
3. Query tags match graph tags; attached edges retain their learned facet information and chunk endpoints. Descriptions and graph relationships participate in retrieval. A topic zero is not an established universal veto on those routes.
4. Named structure and tag-reached graph structure contribute evidence. Outside access survives. Fixed membership sets, unconditional named-first order, one-hop means and strongest-path collapse are not automatically the intended graph process.
5. Both requested facet-use routes remain in scope. Multirank does not require treating head-score gaps as calibrated magnitudes; numerical adjustment requires a justified interpretation of scale and effect. Neither route authorizes equal averaging, an arbitrary cap, or a fixed topic-first hierarchy.
6. Chunk selection retains inspectable supporting tags, edges, descriptions and structural routes. Source bodies are resolved after selection. The shared 72,000-character delivery budget is applied afterward; gold and answer-quality evaluation remain separate from selection.

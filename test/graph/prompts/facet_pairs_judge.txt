You compare two relationships. A relationship is one phrase attached to one text: how that phrase stands to that text. You are given relationship A (phrase A with text A) and relationship B (phrase B with text B). The two texts may be the same text or different texts; the two phrases may be the same phrase or different phrases.

For each of five angles, say which relationship is stronger on that angle: A, B, or equal.

You judge the phrase in the context of its own text, never the phrase by itself and never the text by itself. A phrase that sounds central, temporal, causal, active or specific on its own counts for nothing; only what its own text does with it counts. A text that is full of an angle counts for nothing unless that angle concerns its phrase.

topic — how central the phrase is to what its text is about: if the text were summed up in a sentence or two, how much would the phrase belong in that summary. Not how often it is mentioned.

temporal — how much what the text says about the phrase turns on time: on when things happened, are happening or are due; now, then, soon, before — as against content that would read the same whenever it was written. Not dates for their own sake.

why — how much the text gives the cause or the purpose of the phrase's thing.

activity — how much the phrase's thing is something happening in the text: being done, changed, decided, run — as against being described, referenced or discussed.

concreteness — how specific the text is about the phrase's thing: numbers, names, figures — as against general talk.

Judge only from the two texts in front of you. You may recognise a product, system, company or person named in them; what you know about it from elsewhere is not evidence and must not enter the answer. If a text says little or nothing about its phrase, that relationship is weak on every angle — do not fill the gap.

Answer "equal" when neither relationship is clearly stronger on that angle: when both have none of that angle, and when the two texts do not give you enough to tell them apart. Do not use "equal" to avoid a call — if one side is even slightly stronger on the angle, name that side.
